Research focus.

My research examines why venous thromboembolism occurs, which patients are missed or underrepresented in current evidence, and how routinely collected healthcare data can improve its prevention, detection, and treatment.

01

Mechanisms and determinants of thrombosis

I investigate how genetic susceptibility, coagulation, sleep, infection, diet, and metabolic health contribute to hypercoagulability and thrombotic disease.

Using coagulation phenotyping, thrombin generation, genetics, proteomics, and metabolomics in the NEO Study, UK Biobank, and FinnGen.

02

VTE across populations and healthcare systems

I study how VTE is detected, recorded, and experienced across populations, with particular attention to patients who may be missed by commonly used healthcare-data definitions.

Current work includes VTE validation across Dutch healthcare databases, Korean–Dutch incidence comparisons, and long-term outcomes in older patients.

03

Real-world treatment in high-risk patients

I evaluate the safety and effectiveness of anticoagulant treatment in patients who are often underrepresented in randomized trials.

Current studies focus on severe liver disease, frailty, antiphospholipid syndrome, mechanical heart valves, and infection-related thrombosis.

Methods & data.

A

Linked healthcare data

Nationwide registries, primary-care records, hospital electronic health records, medication dispensing data, and anticoagulation-clinic data.

Methods include phenotype validation, longitudinal analysis, international comparisons, and target-trial emulation.

B

Population cohorts and biobanks

Longitudinal research using the NEO Study, UK Biobank, FinnGen, MEGA, and AT-AGE.

I use cross-cohort replication to assess the robustness and generalizability of epidemiological findings.

C

Hemostasis and multi-omics

Coagulation factors, thrombin generation, genetic variation, proteomics, and metabolomics.

These data are integrated to investigate biological pathways linking thrombosis with cardiometabolic health.
