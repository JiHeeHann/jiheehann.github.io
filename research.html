Research focus.

My research investigates the biological and population-level determinants of venous thromboembolism and examines how epidemiological evidence can improve its prevention, detection, and clinical management.

01

Mechanisms and determinants of thrombosis

I investigate how genetic susceptibility, coagulation, lifestyle, and metabolic health contribute to hypercoagulability and thrombotic disease.

This work combines coagulation phenotyping, thrombin generation, genetics, proteomics, and metabolomics in large population studies.

02

VTE in populations

I study the occurrence, detection, and long-term consequences of venous thromboembolism across populations and healthcare settings.

A particular interest is how differences in populations and data sources influence estimates of VTE incidence and prognosis.

03

Real-world clinical evidence

I use routinely collected healthcare data to study anticoagulant treatment, clinical outcomes, and patterns of care in everyday practice.

This research aims to strengthen the evidence available for clinically complex and underrepresented patient populations.

Methods & data.

A

Healthcare data

Population registries, electronic health records, medication data, and other routinely collected sources of healthcare information.

I apply longitudinal and comparative epidemiological methods to generate reliable evidence from real-world clinical practice.

B

Population cohorts and biobanks

Longitudinal research using the NEO Study, UK Biobank, and FinnGen.

I use cross-cohort replication to assess the robustness and generalizability of epidemiological findings.

C

Hemostasis and multi-omics

Coagulation factors, thrombin generation, genetic variation, proteomics, and metabolomics.

These data are integrated to investigate biological pathways linking thrombosis with cardiometabolic health.
